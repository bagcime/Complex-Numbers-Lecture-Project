1. Introduction to Complex Numbers

1.1 Definition

A complex number is defined as $$ z \;=\; a \;+\; j\,b,\quad a,\,b \in \mathbb{R},\quad j^2 \;=\; -1. $$ Here:

  • \(\;a\;\) is called the real part.
  • \(\;b\;\) is called the imaginary part.
  • We write often \(\Re\{z\}\;=\;a\), \(\Im\{z\}\;=\;b\).

1.2 Real & Imaginary Parts

For any \(z = a + j\,b\):

  • Real part: \(\displaystyle \Re\{z\} = a.\)
  • Imaginary part: \(\displaystyle \Im\{z\} = b.\)
Equivalently, we write \[ z = a + j\,b \;=\; \Re\{z\} \;+\; j\,\Im\{z\}. \]

1.3 Argand Diagram

We can identify each complex number \(z = a + j\,b\) with the point \((a,b)\) in the plane. That 2D‐plane is called the Argand diagram (or complex plane).

Key geometric ideas:

  • Plot \(z = a + j\,b\) at coordinate \((a,b)\).
  • The horizontal axis is the real axis; the vertical axis is the imaginary axis.
  • Geometric operations (e.g.\ addition) become vector‐based. We will see more in Section 3.

2. Rectangular Form

When working in rectangular form, we keep complex numbers as \(a + j\,b\). Addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division are easiest in this form.

2.1 Addition / Subtraction

If \[ z_1 = a + j\,b \quad\text{and}\quad z_2 = c + j\,d, \] then \[ \begin{aligned} z_1 + z_2 &= (a + j\,b) + (c + j\,d) = (a + c) \;+\; j\,(b + d), \\[6pt] z_1 - z_2 &= (a + j\,b) - (c + j\,d) = (a - c) \;+\; j\,(b - d). \end{aligned} \]

Example: \[ (4 + 3j)\;-\;(2 - 6j) \;=\; (4 - 2) \;+\; j\bigl(3 - (-6)\bigr) \;=\; 2 \;+\; j\,9. \]

🎥 Watch: Adding Complex Numbers (Khan Academy)

2.2 Multiplication

Again let \(z_1 = a + j\,b\) and \(z_2 = c + j\,d\). Then \[ z_1 \times z_2 = (a + j\,b)\,(c + j\,d) = (a\,c - b\,d) \;+\; j\,\bigl(a\,d + b\,c\bigr). \]

Example: \[ (1 + 2j)\,(4 + 6j) = \bigl(1 \cdot 4 - 2 \cdot 6\bigr) + j\,\bigl(1 \cdot 6 + 2 \cdot 4\bigr) = (4 - 12) + j\,(6 + 8) = -8 + j\,14. \]

Alternatively, if you already know the polar/phasor form of each factor, you can multiply magnitudes and add angles (see Section 3). But purely in rectangular form, just use the “\(ac - bd\)” (real part) and “\(ad + bc\)” (imaginary part) rule shown above.

🎥 Watch: Multiplying Complex Numbers (Khan Academy)

2.3 Division

To compute \(\displaystyle \frac{z_1}{z_2}\) with \(z_1 = a + j\,b\), \(z_2 = c + j\,d\), multiply numerator and denominator by the conjugate of \(z_2\): \[ \begin{aligned} \frac{a + j\,b}{\,c + j\,d\,} &= \frac{(a + j\,b)\,(c - j\,d)}{(c + j\,d)\,(c - j\,d)} = \frac{(a\,c + b\,d) \;+\; j\,(b\,c - a\,d)}{c^2 + d^2}. \end{aligned} \]

Example: \[ \frac{2 + 4j}{6 - 7j} = \frac{(2 + 4j)\,(6 + 7j)}{(6 - 7j)\,(6 + 7j)} = \frac{12 - 28 + j\,(24 - 14)}{36 + 49} = \frac{-16 + j\,10}{85} = \frac{-16}{85} + j\,\frac{2}{17}. \]

3. Magnitude & Argument (Polar Form)

In polar (or phasor) form, we express \(z = a + j\,b\) by its magnitude \(\lvert z \rvert\) and angle \(\theta = \arg(z)\). Converting back and forth will be covered below.

3.1 Modulus (Magnitude)

If \(z = a + j\,b\), then the modulus (or absolute value) is $$ \lvert z \rvert = \sqrt{\,a^2 + b^2\,}. $$ Geometrically, this is the distance from the origin \((0,0)\) to the point \((a,b)\) in the Argand plane.

🎥 Watch: Absolute Value & Angle (Khan Academy)

3.2 Argument (Angle)

The argument \(\theta = \arg(z)\) is the angle (in radians or degrees) that the vector from the origin to \((a,b)\) makes with the positive real axis. Formally, $$ \theta = \tan^{-1}\!\biggl(\frac{b}{a}\biggr), $$ but you must pay attention to the correct quadrant depending on the signs of \(a\) and \(b\). For example:

  • If \(a > 0\), then \(\theta = \tan^{-1}(b/a)\) lies in \((-90^\circ, +90^\circ)\).
  • If \(a < 0\) and \(b \ge 0\), then \(\theta = \tan^{-1}(b/a) + 180^\circ.\)
  • If \(a < 0\) and \(b < 0\), then \(\theta = \tan^{-1}(b/a) - 180^\circ.\)
  • If \(a = 0\) and \(b > 0\), then \(\theta = +90^\circ.\) If \(a = 0\) and \(b < 0\), then \(\theta = -90^\circ.\)

🎥 Watch: Argument of a Complex Number (Khan Academy)

3.3 Polar Form

Once you know \(\lvert z \rvert\) and \(\theta = \arg(z)\), you can write: $$ z = a + j\,b = \lvert z \rvert \bigl(\cos\theta + j\,\sin\theta\bigr). $$ We often abbreviate this as the “phasor” or “angle” notation: $$ z = \lvert z \rvert \;\angle \theta. $$ Equivalently, by Euler’s formula, $$ z \;=\; \lvert z \rvert \;e^{\,j\theta}, \quad\text{since }e^{j\theta} = \cos\theta + j\,\sin\theta. $$

Examples (Rectangular → Polar):

  • \(4 + 4j\).
    $$ \lvert 4 + 4j \rvert \;=\; \sqrt{4^2 + 4^2} \;=\; \sqrt{32} \;=\; 4\sqrt{2}, \quad \theta = \tan^{-1}\!\bigl(\tfrac{4}{4}\bigr) = 45^\circ. $$ Therefore, \(4 + 4j = 4\sqrt{2}\;\angle 45^\circ.\)
  • \(3 + 0j\).
    $$ \lvert 3 \rvert = 3,\quad \theta = 0^\circ \;\Longrightarrow\; 3 = 3 \;\angle 0^\circ. $$
  • \(-2j\).
    $$ \lvert -2j \rvert = 2,\quad \arg(-2j) = -90^\circ \;\Longrightarrow\; -2j = 2 \;\angle(-90^\circ). $$
  • \(-12 + 3j\).
    $$ \lvert -12 + 3j \rvert = \sqrt{(-12)^2 + 3^2} = \sqrt{144 + 9} = \sqrt{153}, $$ $$ \theta = \tan^{-1}\!\Bigl(\tfrac{3}{-12}\Bigr). \quad \text{Since }a<0,b>0,\;\theta = 180^\circ - 14.036^\circ \approx 165.964^\circ. $$ Hence, $$ -12 + 3j = \sqrt{153}\;\angle 165.96^\circ. $$

🎥 Watch: Polar Form Explanation (Khan Academy)

3.4 Multiplication and Division (Polar Form)

Multiplication and division of complex numbers become very simple in polar form. Given two complex numbers: $$ z_1 = r_1 \angle \theta_1, \quad z_2 = r_2 \angle \theta_2, $$ then:

  • Multiplication: $$ z_1 \cdot z_2 = r_1 r_2 \;\angle\; (\theta_1 + \theta_2) $$
  • Division: $$ \frac{z_1}{z_2} = \frac{r_1}{r_2} \;\angle\; (\theta_1 - \theta_2) $$

Example:

$$ z_1 = 2 \angle 30^\circ,\quad z_2 = 3 \angle 60^\circ $$
  • \( z_1 \cdot z_2 = 6 \;\angle\; 90^\circ \)
  • \( \dfrac{z_1}{z_2} = \dfrac{2}{3} \;\angle\; -30^\circ \)

4. Complex Conjugate

For a complex number \( z = a + j\,b \), its complex conjugate (denoted \( z^*\) or \(\overline{z}\)) is $$ z^* \;=\; a - j\,b. $$

In polar form, if \(z = r\,e^{\,j\theta}\), then $$ z^* = r\,e^{-\,j\theta}. $$

Key properties of the conjugate (for any two complex numbers \(z_1\) and \(z_2\)):

  • \(\;(z_1 + z_2)^* = z_1^* + z_2^*.\)
  • \(\;(z_1 z_2)^* = z_1^* \;z_2^*.\)
  • \(\;\bigl(\tfrac{z_1}{z_2}\bigr)^* = \tfrac{z_1^*}{z_2^*} \quad\) (assuming \(z_2 \neq 0\)).
  • \(\;z \cdot z^* = a^2 + b^2 = \lvert z \rvert^2.\)
✎ Example

Let \(z = -1 + 2j\). Then:

$$ z = -1 + 2j \quad\Longrightarrow\quad z^* = -1 - 2j, $$ $$ \lvert z \rvert^2 = (-1)^2 + (2)^2 = 1 + 4 = 5, \quad z\,z^* = (-1 + 2j)(-1 - 2j) = 5. $$

5. Finding Complex Roots

To find all \(n\)th roots of a complex number \( z = r\,e^{\,j\theta}\), we use:

$$ z^{1/n} = r^{1/n} \;e^{\,j\,\frac{\theta + 2\pi k}{n}} \quad\text{for }k = 0,1,\dots,n-1. $$
✎ Example

Find all cube roots of \(8j\):

$$ \begin{aligned} 8j &= 8\,e^{\,j\pi/2}, \\[6pt] (8j)^{1/3} &= 2\,e^{\,j\;\frac{\pi/2 + 2\pi k}{3}} \quad k = 0,1,2, \\[6pt] &= \begin{cases} 2\,e^{\,j\,\frac{\pi}{6}} = 2\bigl(\cos\tfrac{\pi}{6} + j\sin\tfrac{\pi}{6}\bigr) = \sqrt{3} + j, \\[4pt] 2\,e^{\,j\,\frac{5\pi}{6}} = 2\bigl(\cos\tfrac{5\pi}{6} + j\sin\tfrac{5\pi}{6}\bigr) = -\sqrt{3} + j, \\[4pt] 2\,e^{\,j\,\frac{3\pi}{2}} = 2\bigl(\cos\tfrac{3\pi}{2} + j\sin\tfrac{3\pi}{2}\bigr) = -\,2j. \end{cases} \end{aligned} $$

6. Practice Problems

Test your understanding with these exercises. Solutions are hidden by default – try solving them first!

Problem 1: Basic Operations

Compute \((2 - 3j)(4 + 5j) + \dfrac{1}{j}\).

Solution:

$$ \begin{aligned} (2 - 3j)(4 + 5j) &= 8 + 10j - 12j - 15\,j^2 \\[6pt] &= 8 - 2j + 15 = 23 - 2j, \\[6pt] \frac{1}{j} &= \frac{1}{j} \cdot \frac{j}{j} = \frac{j}{\,j^2\,} = -\,j, \\[6pt] \text{Result} &= 23 - 2j - j = 23 - 3j. \end{aligned} $$

Problem 2: Polar Form

Express \(1 - \sqrt{3}j\) in polar form and compute its cube.

Solution:

$$ \begin{aligned} r &= \sqrt{1^2 + (-\sqrt{3})^2} = 2, \\[6pt] \theta &= \arctan\bigl(\tfrac{-\sqrt{3}}{1}\bigr) = -\,\tfrac{\pi}{3}, \\[6pt] z &= 2\,e^{-\,j\pi/3}, \\[6pt] z^3 &= 2^3 \,e^{-\,j\pi} = 8\,(\,-1 + 0\,j\,) = -8. \end{aligned} $$

Problem 3: Roots of Unity

Find all 4th roots of \(-16\) and plot them in the complex plane.

Solution:

$$ \begin{aligned} -16 &= 16\,e^{\,j\pi}, \\[6pt] (-16)^{1/4} &= 2\,e^{\,j(\,\pi + 2\pi k\,)/4} \quad k = 0,1,2,3, \\[6pt] &= \begin{cases} 2\,e^{\,j\pi/4} = \sqrt{2}\,\bigl(1 + j\bigr), \\[4pt] 2\,e^{\,j3\pi/4} = \sqrt{2}\,\bigl(-1 + j\bigr), \\[4pt] 2\,e^{\,j5\pi/4} = \sqrt{2}\,\bigl(-1 - j\bigr), \\[4pt] 2\,e^{\,j7\pi/4} = \sqrt{2}\,\bigl(1 - j\bigr). \end{cases} \end{aligned} $$

Problem 4: Use the Conjugate

Given \(z = -1 + 2j\), compute its complex conjugate \(z^*\) and verify that \(z \cdot z^* = |z|^2\).

Solution:

$$ \begin{aligned} z &= -1 + 2j, \\[6pt] z^* &= -1 - 2j, \\[6pt] |z|^2 &= (-1)^2 + (2)^2 = 1 + 4 = 5, \\[6pt] z \cdot z^* &= (-1 + 2j)(-1 - 2j) = (-1)(-1) - (2)(-2) + j\bigl[(-1)(-2) + 2(-1)\bigr] \\[4pt] &= (1 - (-4)) + j(2 - 2) = (1 + 4) + j(0) = 5 = |z|^2. \end{aligned} $$

Problem 5: Convert to Polar & Back

Convert \(-3 - 3j\) to polar form, then express it back in rectangular form from your polar result.

Solution:

$$ \begin{aligned} -3 - 3j &: \quad r = \sqrt{(-3)^2 + (-3)^2} = \sqrt{18} = 3\sqrt{2}, \\[6pt] &\quad \theta = \tan^{-1}\!\Bigl(\frac{-3}{-3}\Bigr) = \tan^{-1}(1) = 45^\circ, \\ &\quad \text{but both }a<0,\,b<0 \text{, so } \theta = 45^\circ - 180^\circ = -135^\circ. \\[6pt] \text{Polar form:} &\quad 3\sqrt{2}\;\angle(-135^\circ). \\[6pt] \text{Back to rectangular:} &\quad 3\sqrt{2}\bigl(\cos(-135^\circ) + j\sin(-135^\circ)\bigr) \\[4pt] &= 3\sqrt{2}\bigl(-\tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} - j\,\tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\bigr) = -3 - 3j. \end{aligned} $$

Problem 6: Simplify in Phasor Form

Simplify the following expression in phasor form:
\(\displaystyle \frac{(3 + 4j)(1 - 2j)}{2 - j}\).

Solution:

$$ \begin{aligned} (3 + 4j)(1 - 2j) &= (3 \cdot 1 - 4 \cdot (-2)) + j(3 \cdot (-2) + 4 \cdot 1) = (3 + 8) + j(-6 + 4) = 11 - 2j, \\[6pt] \frac{11 - 2j}{2 - j} &= \frac{(11 - 2j)(2 + j)}{(2 - j)(2 + j)} = \frac{22 + 11j - 4j - 2\,j^2}{4 + 1} = \frac{22 + 7j + 2}{5} = \frac{24 + 7j}{5} = 4.8 + 1.4j. \end{aligned} $$ Converting \(4.8 + 1.4j\) to phasor: $$ r = \sqrt{4.8^2 + 1.4^2} \approx \sqrt{23.04 + 1.96} = \sqrt{25} = 5, $$ $$ \theta = \tan^{-1}\!\Bigl(\frac{1.4}{4.8}\Bigr) \approx \tan^{-1}(0.2917) \approx 16.26^\circ. $$ Thus the phasor form is $$5 \;\angle 16.26^\circ.$$

Problem 7: Power of a Complex Number

Compute \((1 + i)^5\) and express the result in standard form \(a + bi\).

Solution:

$$ \begin{aligned} 1 + j &= \sqrt{2}\,e^{\,j\pi/4}, \\[6pt] (1 + j)^5 &= \bigl(\sqrt{2}\bigr)^5 \, e^{\,j\,5(\pi/4)} = 2^{5/2} \, e^{\,j\,5\pi/4}. \\[6pt] 2^{5/2} &= 4\sqrt{2}, \quad 5\pi/4 = \pi + \pi/4, \\[6pt] e^{\,j\,5\pi/4} &= \cos\bigl(\tfrac{5\pi}{4}\bigr) + j\sin\bigl(\tfrac{5\pi}{4}\bigr) = \bigl(-\tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\bigr) + j\bigl(-\tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\bigr). \\[6pt] \therefore\;(1 + j)^5 &= 4\sqrt{2} \,\Bigl(-\tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} - j\,\tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\Bigr) = 4\sqrt{2} \,\Bigl(-\tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\Bigr) \;+\; j\,4\sqrt{2}\,\Bigl(-\tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\Bigr) \\[6pt] &= 4(-1) + j\,4(-1) = -4 - 4j. \end{aligned} $$

Problem 8: Conjugate of a Sum and Product

Let \(z_1 = 2 - 3j\) and \(z_2 = -1 + 4j\). Verify that \((z_1 + z_2)^* = z_1^* + z_2^*\) and \((z_1 z_2)^* = z_1^*\,z_2^*\).

Solution:

$$ \begin{aligned} z_1 + z_2 &= (2 - 3j) + (-1 + 4j) = (2 - 1) + j(-3 + 4) = 1 + j, \\[6pt] (z_1 + z_2)^* &= (1 + j)^* = 1 - j, \\[6pt] z_1^* + z_2^* &= (2 + 3j) + (-1 - 4j) = (2 - 1) + j(3 - 4) = 1 - j. \\[12pt] z_1 z_2 &= (2 - 3j)(-1 + 4j) = 2(-1) + 2\cdot 4j - 3j(-1) - 3j\cdot 4j \\[6pt] &= -2 + 8j + 3j - 12\,j^2 = -2 + 11j + 12 = 10 + 11j, \\[6pt] (z_1 z_2)^* &= (10 + 11j)^* = 10 - 11j, \\[6pt] z_1^*\,z_2^* &= (2 + 3j)(-1 - 4j) = 2(-1) + 2(-4j) + 3j(-1) + 3j(-4j) \\[6pt] &= -2 - 8j - 3j - 12\,j^2 = -2 - 11j + 12 = 10 - 11j. \end{aligned} $$ Both identities hold.

Problem 9: Argument of a Complex Number

Find the argument \(\arg(z)\) of \(z = -1 - j\) in the range \((-\pi, \pi]\).

Solution:

$$ \begin{aligned} z &= -1 - j, \quad a = -1,\; b = -1, \\[6pt] r &= \sqrt{(-1)^2 + (-1)^2} = \sqrt{2}, \\[6pt] \theta &= \tan^{-1}\!\Bigl(\frac{-1}{-1}\Bigr) = \tan^{-1}(1) = \frac{\pi}{4}, \\[6pt] \text{But }a<0,\;b<0 \implies \theta = -\pi + \frac{\pi}{4} = -\frac{3\pi}{4}. \\[6pt] \therefore\; \arg(-1 - j) &= -\frac{3\pi}{4}. \end{aligned} $$

Problem 10: Division of Complex Numbers

Compute \(\displaystyle \frac{5 - 2j}{1 + 3j}\) and express the answer in the form \(a + bj\).

Solution:

$$ \begin{aligned} \frac{5 - 2j}{1 + 3j} &= \frac{(5 - 2j)(1 - 3j)}{(1 + 3j)(1 - 3j)} = \frac{5(1) - 5(3j) - 2j(1) + 2j(3j)}{1^2 + 3^2} \\[6pt] &= \frac{5 - 15j - 2j + 6\,j^2}{1 + 9} = \frac{5 - 17j + 6(-1)}{10} = \frac{-1 - 17j}{10} = -\frac{1}{10} - \frac{17}{10}j. \end{aligned} $$

Problem 11: Solving a Quadratic in \(z\)

Solve the equation \(z^2 = -1 + \sqrt{3}\,j\) for all complex solutions \(z\).

Solution:

$$ \begin{aligned} -1 + \sqrt{3}\,j &= 2\,e^{\,j\,\frac{2\pi}{3}}, \quad\text{(since }r = \sqrt{(-1)^2 + (\sqrt{3})^2} = 2,\; \theta = \tan^{-1}\!\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{-1} = \frac{2\pi}{3}\text{)}, \\[6pt] z^2 &= 2\,e^{\,j\,\frac{2\pi}{3}} \;\Longrightarrow\; z = \sqrt{2}\,e^{\,j\,\frac{1}{2}\,( \frac{2\pi}{3} + 2\pi k )}, \quad k = 0,1. \\[6pt] \text{For }k=0: &\quad z = \sqrt{2}\,e^{\,j\,\frac{\pi}{3}} = \sqrt{2}\Bigl(\cos\frac{\pi}{3} + j\sin\frac{\pi}{3}\Bigr) = \sqrt{2}\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{2} + j\,\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\Bigr) = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} + j\,\frac{\sqrt{6}}{2}. \\[6pt] \text{For }k=1: &\quad z = \sqrt{2}\,e^{\,j\,\frac{1}{2}\,(\frac{2\pi}{3} + 2\pi)} = \sqrt{2}\,e^{\,j\,\frac{7\pi}{3}} = \sqrt{2}\,e^{\,j\,( \frac{7\pi}{3} - 2\pi )} = \sqrt{2}\,e^{\,j\,\frac{\pi}{3}} \text{(same as }k=0\text{)}. \\[3pt] \text{Actually, the distinct angles are } \frac{\pi}{3}, \; \frac{\pi}{3} + \pi = \frac{4\pi}{3}. \\[6pt] \Rightarrow\; z &= \begin{cases} \sqrt{2}\bigl(\cos\tfrac{\pi}{3} + j\sin\tfrac{\pi}{3}\bigr) = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} + j\,\frac{\sqrt{6}}{2}, \\[4pt] \sqrt{2}\bigl(\cos\tfrac{4\pi}{3} + j\sin\tfrac{4\pi}{3}\bigr) = \sqrt{2}\Bigl(-\tfrac{1}{2} - j\,\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\Bigr) = -\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} - j\,\frac{\sqrt{6}}{2}. \end{cases} \end{aligned} $$

Problem 12: Euler’s Identity Check

Show that \(e^{\,j\pi/4} = \cos\!\bigl(\tfrac{\pi}{4}\bigr) + j\sin\!\bigl(\tfrac{\pi}{4}\bigr)\) and compute its rectangular form.

Solution:

$$ \begin{aligned} e^{\,j\pi/4} &= \cos\!\Bigl(\tfrac{\pi}{4}\Bigr) + j\sin\!\Bigl(\tfrac{\pi}{4}\Bigr), \quad \text{(Euler’s formula)} \\[6pt] \cos\!\Bigl(\tfrac{\pi}{4}\Bigr) &= \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}, \quad \sin\!\Bigl(\tfrac{\pi}{4}\Bigr) = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}, \\[6pt] \therefore\; e^{\,j\pi/4} &= \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} + j\,\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}. \end{aligned} $$

Problem 13: Triangle Inequality Example

Let \(z_1 = 3 + 4j\) and \(z_2 = -2 + j\). Verify numerically that \(\lvert z_1 + z_2\rvert \le \lvert z_1\rvert + \lvert z_2\rvert\).

Solution:

$$ \begin{aligned} z_1 + z_2 &= (3 + 4j) + (-2 + j) = (3 - 2) + j(4 + 1) = 1 + 5j, \\[6pt] \lvert z_1 + z_2\rvert &= \sqrt{1^2 + 5^2} = \sqrt{26}, \\[6pt] \lvert z_1\rvert &= \sqrt{3^2 + 4^2} = 5, \quad \lvert z_2\rvert = \sqrt{(-2)^2 + 1^2} = \sqrt{5}, \\[6pt] \lvert z_1\rvert + \lvert z_2\rvert &= 5 + \sqrt{5}. \end{aligned} $$ Numerically: $$ \sqrt{26} \approx 5.099, \quad 5 + \sqrt{5} \approx 5 + 2.236 = 7.236. $$ Hence $$ \lvert z_1 + z_2\rvert \approx 5.099 \;\le\; 7.236 = \lvert z_1\rvert + \lvert z_2\rvert, $$ verifying the triangle inequality.

Problem 14: Inverse of a Complex Number

Find the multiplicative inverse \(z^{-1}\) of \(z = 4 - 3j\) in the form \(a + bj\).

Solution:

$$ \begin{aligned} z &= 4 - 3j, \quad z^{-1} = \frac{1}{4 - 3j} = \frac{4 + 3j}{(4 - 3j)(4 + 3j)} \\[6pt] &= \frac{4 + 3j}{4^2 + 3^2} = \frac{4 + 3j}{16 + 9} = \frac{4 + 3j}{25} = \frac{4}{25} + j\,\frac{3}{25}. \end{aligned} $$

Problem 15: Fifth Roots of Unity

List all the 5th roots of unity (solutions of \(z^5 = 1\)) and express them in trigonometric form.

Solution:

$$ \begin{aligned} z^5 &= 1 = e^{\,j\,2\pi m}, \; m = 0,1,2,3,4 \\[6pt] z_k &= e^{\,j\,\frac{2\pi k}{5}}, \quad k = 0,1,2,3,4. \\[6pt] \text{Hence:} \begin{cases} z_0 = e^{\,j\,0} = 1, \\[4pt] z_1 = e^{\,j\,\frac{2\pi}{5}} = \cos\!\Bigl(\tfrac{2\pi}{5}\Bigr) + j\sin\!\Bigl(\tfrac{2\pi}{5}\Bigr), \\[4pt] z_2 = e^{\,j\,\frac{4\pi}{5}} = \cos\!\Bigl(\tfrac{4\pi}{5}\Bigr) + j\sin\!\Bigl(\tfrac{4\pi}{5}\Bigr), \\[4pt] z_3 = e^{\,j\,\frac{6\pi}{5}} = \cos\!\Bigl(\tfrac{6\pi}{5}\Bigr) + j\sin\!\Bigl(\tfrac{6\pi}{5}\Bigr), \\[4pt] z_4 = e^{\,j\,\frac{8\pi}{5}} = \cos\!\Bigl(\tfrac{8\pi}{5}\Bigr) + j\sin\!\Bigl(\tfrac{8\pi}{5}\Bigr). \end{cases} \end{aligned} $$

Problem 16: Simplify a Quotient to a Real Number

Simplify \(\displaystyle \frac{1 + j}{1 - j}\) and show that it is purely real.

Solution:

$$ \begin{aligned} \frac{1 + j}{1 - j} &= \frac{(1 + j)(1 + j)}{(1 - j)(1 + j)} = \frac{1 + 2j + j^2}{1 - j^2} = \frac{1 + 2j - 1}{1 - (-1)} \\[6pt] &= \frac{2j}{2} = j. \end{aligned} $$ Oops—this indicates a purely imaginary answer. But if we meant to find a real result, consider: $$ \frac{1 - j}{1 + j} = \frac{(1 - j)(1 - j)}{(1 + j)(1 - j)} = \frac{1 - 2j + j^2}{1 - j^2} = \frac{1 - 2j - 1}{1 - (-1)} = \frac{-2j}{2} = -\,j. $$ Both quotients are purely imaginary. If instead we compute $$ \frac{(1 + j)^2}{(1 - j)^2} = \left(\frac{1 + j}{1 - j}\right)^2 = j^2 = -1, $$ which is purely real.

Problem 17: Sum of Reciprocals

Compute \(\displaystyle \frac{1}{2 + 3j} \;+\; \frac{1}{1 - 2j}\) and express the result in the form \(a + bj\).

Solution:

$$ \begin{aligned} \frac{1}{2 + 3j} &= \frac{2 - 3j}{(2 + 3j)(2 - 3j)} = \frac{2 - 3j}{4 + 9} = \frac{2 - 3j}{13} = \frac{2}{13} - \frac{3}{13}j, \\[6pt] \frac{1}{1 - 2j} &= \frac{1 + 2j}{(1 - 2j)(1 + 2j)} = \frac{1 + 2j}{1 + 4} = \frac{1 + 2j}{5} = \frac{1}{5} + \frac{2}{5}j. \\[6pt] \text{Sum: } &= \Bigl(\tfrac{2}{13} - \tfrac{3}{13}j\Bigr) + \Bigl(\tfrac{1}{5} + \tfrac{2}{5}j\Bigr) = \Bigl(\tfrac{2}{13} + \tfrac{1}{5}\Bigr) + j\Bigl(-\tfrac{3}{13} + \tfrac{2}{5}\Bigr) \\[6pt] &= \Bigl(\tfrac{10 + 13}{65}\Bigr) + j\Bigl(\tfrac{-15 + 26}{65}\Bigr) = \frac{23}{65} + j\,\frac{11}{65}. \end{aligned} $$

Problem 18: Real Part of a Product

Let \(z_1 = a + bj\) and \(z_2 = c + dj\). Show that \(\Re\{\,z_1\,\overline{z_2}\} = ac + bd\).

Solution:

$$ \begin{aligned} z_1\,\overline{z_2} &= (a + bj)(c - dj) = a\,c - a\,d\,j + b j\,c - b j\,d\,j \\[6pt] &= a\,c - a\,d\,j + b\,c\,j - b\,d\,j^2 = a\,c - a\,d\,j + b\,c\,j + b\,d \\[6pt] &= (a\,c + b\,d) + j\,(b\,c - a\,d). \\[6pt] \text{Therefore, } \Re\{\,z_1\,\overline{z_2}\} = a\,c + b\,d. \end{aligned} $$

Problem 19: Magnitude of a Product

Compute \(\lvert (3 - 4j)\,(1 + j)\rvert\) using rectangular form and compare with \(\lvert 3 - 4j\rvert \,\lvert 1 + j\rvert\).

Solution:

$$ \begin{aligned} (3 - 4j)(1 + j) &= 3(1) + 3j - 4j(1) - 4j^2 = 3 + 3j - 4j + 4 = 7 - j. \\[6pt] \lvert 7 - j\rvert &= \sqrt{7^2 + (-1)^2} = \sqrt{49 + 1} = \sqrt{50} = 5\sqrt{2}. \\[6pt] \lvert 3 - 4j\rvert &= \sqrt{3^2 + (-4)^2} = \sqrt{9 + 16} = 5, \\[6pt] \lvert 1 + j\rvert &= \sqrt{1^2 + 1^2} = \sqrt{2}. \\[6pt] \lvert 3 - 4j\rvert \,\lvert 1 + j\rvert &= 5 \times \sqrt{2} = 5\sqrt{2}, \quad \text{which matches } \lvert 7 - j\rvert. \end{aligned} $$

Problem 20: Argument in Correct Quadrant

Find \(\arg(z)\) for \(z = -\sqrt{3} + j\) in the range \((-\pi, \pi]\).

Solution:

$$ \begin{aligned} z &= -\sqrt{3} + j, \quad a = -\sqrt{3},\; b = 1. \\[6pt] r &= \sqrt{(\!-\sqrt{3})^2 + 1^2} = \sqrt{3 + 1} = 2, \\[6pt] \theta_0 &= \tan^{-1}\!\Bigl(\frac{b}{a}\Bigr) = \tan^{-1}\!\Bigl(\frac{1}{-\sqrt{3}}\Bigr) = \tan^{-1}\!\bigl(-\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\bigr) = -\frac{\pi}{6}, \\[6pt] \text{But }a<0,\,b>0 \implies \theta = \pi - \frac{\pi}{6} = \frac{5\pi}{6}. \\[6pt] \therefore\;\arg\bigl(-\sqrt{3} + j\bigr) &= \frac{5\pi}{6}. \end{aligned} $$

Problem 21: Sixth Roots of \(-1\)

Find all solutions of \(z^6 = -1\) and express them in exponential form \(re^{j\theta}\).

Solution:

$$ \begin{aligned} -1 &= e^{\,j\pi}, \quad z^6 = e^{\,j\pi} \;\Longrightarrow\; z = e^{\,j\,\frac{\pi + 2\pi k}{6}}, \quad k = 0,1,2,3,4,5. \\[6pt] \text{Hence: } z_k &= e^{\,j\,\frac{\pi + 2\pi k}{6}} = e^{\,j\,\frac{(2k + 1)\pi}{6}}, \quad k = 0,1,2,3,4,5. \\[6pt] \text{Explicitly: } \begin{cases} e^{\,j\,\frac{\pi}{6}}, \\[4pt] e^{\,j\,\frac{3\pi}{6}} = e^{\,j\,\frac{\pi}{2}}, \\[4pt] e^{\,j\,\frac{5\pi}{6}}, \\[4pt] e^{\,j\,\frac{7\pi}{6}}, \\[4pt] e^{\,j\,\frac{9\pi}{6}} = e^{\,j\,\frac{3\pi}{2}}, \\[4pt] e^{\,j\,\frac{11\pi}{6}}. \end{cases} \end{aligned} $$

Problem 22: Squared Magnitude Identity

Show that for any complex \(z\), \(\lvert z\rvert^2 = z\,\overline{z}\), and apply it to \(z = 1 - 2j\).

Solution:

$$ \begin{aligned} z &= x + yj, \quad \overline{z} = x - yj, \\[6pt] z\,\overline{z} &= (x + yj)(x - yj) = x^2 - x\,yj + x\,yj - y^2\,j^2 = x^2 + y^2. \\[6pt] \lvert z\rvert^2 &= x^2 + y^2 \;=\; z\,\overline{z}. \\[12pt] \text{For } z = 1 - 2j: x = 1,\; y = -2, \\[6pt] z\,\overline{z} = (1 - 2j)(1 + 2j) = 1 + 2j - 2j - 4\,j^2 = 1 + 4 = 5, \\[6pt] \lvert 1 - 2j\rvert^2 &= 1^2 + (-2)^2 = 1 + 4 = 5. \end{aligned} $$

Problem 23: Addition in Polar Form

Evaluate \(z_1 + z_2\) if \(z_1 = 5\angle 30^\circ\) and \(z_2 = 3\angle 120^\circ\). Express the result in rectangular form \(a + bj\).

Solution:

$$ \begin{aligned} z_1 &= 5\Bigl(\cos 30^\circ + j\sin 30^\circ\Bigr) = 5\Bigl(\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} + j\,\tfrac{1}{2}\Bigr) = \frac{5\sqrt{3}}{2} + j\,\frac{5}{2}, \\[6pt] z_2 &= 3\Bigl(\cos 120^\circ + j\sin 120^\circ\Bigr) = 3\Bigl(-\tfrac{1}{2} + j\,\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\Bigr) = -\tfrac{3}{2} + j\,\tfrac{3\sqrt{3}}{2}. \\[6pt] z_1 + z_2 &= \Bigl(\frac{5\sqrt{3}}{2} - \frac{3}{2}\Bigr) + j\Bigl(\frac{5}{2} + \frac{3\sqrt{3}}{2}\Bigr). \\[6pt] &= \frac{5\sqrt{3} - 3}{2} \;+\; j\,\frac{5 + 3\sqrt{3}}{2}. \end{aligned} $$

Problem 24: Solving a Cubic in \(z\)

Solve \(z^3 = 8\) for all complex solutions \(z\).

Solution:

$$ \begin{aligned} 8 &= 8\,e^{\,j\,0}, \quad z^3 = 8\,e^{\,j\,0} \;\Longrightarrow\; z = 2\,e^{\,j\,\frac{2\pi k}{3}}, \quad k = 0,1,2. \\[6pt] \text{Thus:} \begin{cases} z_0 = 2\,e^{\,j\,0} = 2, \\[4pt] z_1 = 2\,e^{\,j\,\frac{2\pi}{3}} = 2\Bigl(-\tfrac{1}{2} + j\,\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\Bigr) = -1 + j\sqrt{3}, \\[4pt] z_2 = 2\,e^{\,j\,\frac{4\pi}{3}} = 2\Bigl(-\tfrac{1}{2} - j\,\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\Bigr) = -1 - j\sqrt{3}. \end{cases} \end{aligned} $$

Problem 25: Inverse and Verification

Find the inverse \(z^{-1}\) of \(z = -2 + j\) and verify by checking \(z \cdot z^{-1} = 1\).

Solution:

$$ \begin{aligned} z &= -2 + j, \quad z^{-1} = \frac{1}{-2 + j} = \frac{-2 - j}{(-2 + j)(-2 - j)} \\[6pt] &= \frac{-2 - j}{4 + 1} = \frac{-2 - j}{5} = -\frac{2}{5} - \frac{1}{5}j. \\[6pt] z \cdot z^{-1} &= (-2 + j)\Bigl(-\tfrac{2}{5} - \tfrac{1}{5}j\Bigr) = (-2)\Bigl(-\tfrac{2}{5}\Bigr) + (-2)\Bigl(-\tfrac{1}{5}j\Bigr) + j\Bigl(-\tfrac{2}{5}\Bigr) + j\Bigl(-\tfrac{1}{5}j\Bigr) \\[6pt] &= \frac{4}{5} + \frac{2}{5}j - \frac{2}{5}j - \frac{1}{5}j^2 = \frac{4}{5} + 0 + \frac{1}{5} = 1. \end{aligned} $$

Problem 26: Geometric Rotation

Let \(z = 1 + j\). Compute \(w = j\,z\). Describe geometrically how \(w\) is obtained from \(z\) in the complex plane.

Solution:

$$ \begin{aligned} z &= 1 + j, \\[6pt] w &= j\,z = j(1 + j) = j + j^2 = j - 1 = -1 + j. \\[6pt] \text{In the Argand plane, multiplying by }j \text{ rotates a point }90^\circ\text{ counterclockwise.} \\[4pt] z = (1,1) \quad\longrightarrow\quad w = (-1,1). \end{aligned} $$ Thus, \(w\) is \(z\) rotated \(90^\circ\) CCW about the origin.

Problem 27: Identify Real & Imaginary Parts

For \(z = -5 + 7j\), state \(\Re\{z\}\) and \(\Im\{z\}\).

Problem 29: Add Two Complex Numbers

Compute \((2 + 3j) + (-1 + 5j)\).

Solution:

$$ \begin{aligned} (2 + 3j) + (-1 + 5j) &= (2 + (-1)) + j(3 + 5) \\[6pt] &= 1 + 8j. \end{aligned} $$

Problem 30: Subtract Two Complex Numbers

Compute \((4 - 2j) - (1 + 3j)\).

Solution:

$$ \begin{aligned} (4 - 2j) - (1 + 3j) &= (4 - 1) + j(-2 - 3) \\[6pt] &= 3 - 5j. \end{aligned} $$

Problem 31: Multiply Two Complex Numbers

Compute \((1 + 2j)\,(3 - j)\).

Solution:

$$ \begin{aligned} (1 + 2j)\,(3 - j) &= 1\cdot 3 + 1\cdot(-j) + 2j\cdot 3 + 2j\cdot(-j) \\[6pt] &= 3 - j + 6j - 2\,j^2 = 3 + 5j - 2(-1) = 3 + 5j + 2 = 5 + 5j. \end{aligned} $$

Problem 32: Divide Two Complex Numbers

Compute \(\displaystyle \frac{5 + j}{2 - j}\) in rectangular form.

Solution:

$$ \begin{aligned} \frac{5 + j}{2 - j} &= \frac{(5 + j)\,(2 + j)}{(2 - j)\,(2 + j)} = \frac{10 + 5j + 2j + j^2}{4 + 1} \\[6pt] &= \frac{10 + 7j - 1}{5} = \frac{9 + 7j}{5} = \frac{9}{5} + \frac{7}{5}j. \end{aligned} $$

Problem 33: Add and Multiply

Given \(z_1 = 2 + j\) and \(z_2 = -3 + 4j\), compute \(z_1 + z_2\) and \(z_1\,z_2\).

Solution:

$$ \begin{aligned} z_1 + z_2 &= (2 + j) + (-3 + 4j) = (2 - 3) + j(1 + 4) = -1 + 5j, \\[6pt] z_1\,z_2 &= (2 + j)\,(-3 + 4j) = 2(-3) + 2\cdot 4j + j(-3) + j(4j) \\[6pt] &= -6 + 8j - 3j + 4\,j^2 = -6 + 5j + 4(-1) = -6 + 5j - 4 = -10 + 5j. \end{aligned} $$

Problem 34: Convert to Rectangular Form

Express \(z = 6\,e^{j\pi}\) in rectangular form \(a + bj\).

Solution:

$$ \begin{aligned} z &= 6\,e^{j\pi} = 6\bigl(\cos\pi + j\sin\pi\bigr) = 6(-1 + j\cdot 0) = -6 + 0\,j = -6. \end{aligned} $$

Problem 35: Verify Zero Condition

Find all \(z\) such that \(\Re\{z\} = 0\) and \(\Im\{z\} = 0\).

Solution:

$$ \begin{aligned} \Re\{z\} = 0 \;\text{and}\; \Im\{z\} = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; z = 0 + 0\,j = 0. \end{aligned} $$ The only complex number with both real and imaginary parts zero is \(z = 0\).

Problem 36: Basic Conjugate Practice

Compute the conjugate of \(z = 4 - 7j\), then verify \(\Re\{z\} = \Re\{\,\overline{z}\}\) and \(\Im\{z\} = -\,\Im\{\,\overline{z}\}\).

Solution:

$$ \begin{aligned} z &= 4 - 7j, \quad \overline{z} = 4 + 7j, \\[6pt] \Re\{z\} &= 4, \quad \Re\{\,\overline{z}\} = 4, \\[6pt] \Im\{z\} &= -7, \quad \Im\{\,\overline{z}\} = 7, \quad \text{so } \Im\{z\} = -\,\Im\{\,\overline{z}\}. \end{aligned} $$
